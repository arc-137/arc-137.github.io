Hardwood at a lumberyard

ThicknessRoughPlaned (S2S)

Random widths and lengths, priced by the board foot. Home stores also sell hardwood planed to ¾″ in 1×4, 1×6 and so on, like pine.

Board feet

One board foot is a piece 12″ × 12″ × 1″ (rough thickness).

thick (in) × wide (in) × long (in) ÷ 144

A 4/4 board 6″ wide and 8 ft long: 1 × 6 × 96 ÷ 144 = 4 board feet.

Buy about 25% more than your parts add up to: random widths and planing waste some.

Prices change by store and season: Arc's shopping list starts with typical ones, so type your store's over them. Arc never looks them up.

Pilot holes for #8 screws

Softwoods (pine, cedar) and poplar: 3⁄32″. Hardwoods and plywood: ⅛″. Clearance through the first board: 11⁄64″. Very hard woods (): drill slower and always pre-drill.

Outdoors

last outside; everything else needs paint or an exterior finish and will still wear. Use stainless or coated screws outdoors.

Stain

go blotchy: use a pre-stain conditioner or a gel stain, and try it on an offcut first. Oak and walnut take stain evenly.

Dust

Wear a dust mask for sanding, and for cutting : their dust can irritate eyes, skin and lungs.

Hardness and weight are typical published values; real boards vary. Check anything that matters with your store or a wood teacher. The pictures are drawn by code: colour and figure change from board to board.
